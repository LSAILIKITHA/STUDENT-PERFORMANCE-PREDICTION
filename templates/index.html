About the Machine Learning Model

Theoretical foundations, Find-S algorithm mechanics, empirical evaluation, and operational limitations.

1. What is Concept Learning?

In machine learning, Concept Learning is defined as inferring a boolean-valued function from training examples of its input and output attributes.

In our educational analytics context, the target concept is:

c(x) = 1 (Good Performer)   vs   c(x) = 0 (Needs Academic Support)

Each student instance $x$ is represented as a conjunction of continuous attributes mapped into discrete concept states:

  • Attendance: Regular (≥75%) vs Irregular (<75%)
  • Internal Assessment: Satisfactory (≥30/50) vs Unsatisfactory (<30/50)
  • Assignment Completion: Completed (≥70%) vs Incomplete (<70%)
  • Study Hours: Sufficient (≥2.5 hrs/day) vs Insufficient (<2.5 hrs/day)
  • Previous Exam: Passed (≥60%) vs BelowAverage (<60%)

2. The Find-S (Find-Specific) Algorithm

Formulated by Tom Mitchell (1997), Find-S traverses the hypothesis space $H$ from the most specific hypothesis toward generalized constraints that encompass all positive examples:

Step 1
Initialize $h$ to the most specific hypothesis: h = ⟨ Ø, Ø, Ø, Ø, Ø ⟩.
Step 2
For each positive training example $x$ ($label = \text{Good Performer}$):
• For each attribute constraint $a_i$ in $h$:
   - If $a_i = \emptyset$, set $a_i = x_i$.
   - Else if $a_i \neq x_i$ and $a_i \neq '?'$, generalize to $a_i = '?'$.
Step 3
Negative training examples are completely ignored.
Step 4
Output the final most specific consistent hypothesis $h$.
